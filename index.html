How to read this

  • WindowOrders placed from January 2017 to August 2018. The data runs from September 2016 to October 2018, but the first four and last two months hold only a few hundred orders, so they are left out.
  • LateAn order is late if it was delivered on a later calendar day than the estimated delivery date. The estimate is a date with no time of day, while the actual delivery has a date and a time. Comparing exact moments would count an order delivered at 3 pm on the estimated day as late, which is unfair. That method would count — orders as late instead of —.
  • Delivered onlyDelivery times, late shares, reviews and revenue use delivered orders. — of orders were canceled or unavailable and are not in those figures.
  • One review per orderA few orders have two or three reviews. The most recent one is used.
  • A customer is a personThe marketplace gives every order a new customer ID, so people are identified by their unique customer ID.
  • Coming backA customer came back if they ordered on a later day than their first order, within 180 days. Several orders on one day count as one trip. Only customers with a full 180 days of follow-up are counted.
  • Only reviewersReview scores come from customers who chose to write one. Customers with a bad experience may be more likely to review.
  • Correlation, not proofLate orders go with low scores, but the data cannot show that lateness caused them. Late orders may also involve damaged goods or distant sellers.
  • Old data in reaisOrders are from 2016 to 2018 and prices are Brazilian reais, so this is a picture of that period.
